Frequently Asked Questions

What is religious horror?
Religious horror is a subgenre that draws its terror from faith, theology, and spiritual belief systems. It explores the dark side of devotion — demonic possession, divine punishment, corrupted clergy, fanatical cults, and the thin line between piety and madness. The genre works because it weaponizes something deeply personal: the viewer's relationship with the sacred.
Why is The Exorcist considered the scariest movie?
The Exorcist endures because it treats its subject with absolute sincerity. Friedkin shot it like a documentary, grounding the supernatural in clinical realism. The possession of Regan isn't played for spectacle — it's presented as a medical crisis that escalates beyond science. The film also taps into a primal fear: that faith might be real, and so might the things faith warns us about.
What's the difference between religious horror and supernatural horror?
All religious horror is supernatural, but not all supernatural horror is religious. Supernatural horror encompasses any film with ghosts, curses, or otherworldly forces. Religious horror specifically roots its terror in organized belief systems — Christianity, paganism, folk religion. The horror comes not just from the existence of evil, but from its relationship to the divine.

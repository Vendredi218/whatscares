Best Folk Horror Movies of All Time

Folk horror draws its dread from ancient rituals, rural isolation, and pagan traditions that never quite died. These films trade haunted houses for harvest fields and demons for something older — the terrifying logic of communities bound to the land and its demands.

Tumbbad

A multi-generational Indian fable that spans decades of one family's greed as they pursue a cursed treasure guarded by a forgotten god. Tumbbad is folk horror at its most mythic — drenched in monsoon rain and rotting grandeur, it proves the subgenre has always been global. Frequently Asked Questions

What is folk horror?
Folk horror is a subgenre that draws on folklore, pagan traditions, and rural superstition to create dread. Rather than jump scares or supernatural monsters, it finds terror in isolated communities, ancient rituals, and the sense that the land itself harbors something old and dangerous. The term was popularized by Mark Gatiss in his 2010 BBC documentary series.
What are the key themes of folk horror?
Folk horror revolves around rural isolation and the tension between outsiders and insular communities; pagan or pre-Christian belief systems persisting beneath a modern surface; sacrificial rituals to appease nature or harvest gods; the landscape as a source of menace; and the erosion of rational thought when characters are immersed in these environments.
What is the folk horror chain?
The folk horror chain is a narrative framework identified by Adam Scovell in his book Folk Horror: Hours Dreadful and Things Strange. It describes a four-link sequence: the landscape, which establishes an isolated setting; isolation, both geographic and cultural; skewed belief systems alien to the protagonist; and the happening, a violent or supernatural climax the previous links have been building toward.
What are the best folk horror movies to start with?
The Wicker Man (1973) is the essential starting point — it practically defined the subgenre. Midsommar (2019) is the best modern entry, transplanting folk horror into sunlit Scandinavian ritual. The Witch (2015) offers a period-piece approach rooted in Puritan-era paranoia. After those three, Tumbbad (2018) provides a stunning non-Western perspective through Indian mythology.

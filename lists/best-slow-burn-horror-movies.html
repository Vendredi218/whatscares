Frequently Asked Questions

What is slow-burn horror?
Slow-burn horror is a subgenre that prioritizes atmosphere, tension, and gradual escalation over jump scares and immediate thrills. These films take their time establishing mood and character, building an almost unbearable sense of dread before delivering a payoff that's all the more devastating for the wait. The focus is on what you don't see as much as what you do.
Are slow-burn horror movies scary?
Slow-burn horror movies trade instant shocks for a deeper, more persistent kind of fear. They may not make you jump in your seat, but they burrow under your skin. Films like Hereditary and Tumbbad build to moments of genuine terror that hit harder because of the careful setup. The fear tends to follow you home rather than ending with the credits.
What's the difference between slow-burn and art house horror?
There's significant overlap, but not all slow-burn horror is arthouse and vice versa. Slow-burn refers specifically to pacing — a deliberate, escalating structure. Arthouse horror is more about artistic ambition, unconventional narrative, and thematic depth. A film like Bone Tomahawk is slow-burn but firmly genre; Under the Skin is both arthouse and slow-burn.
